---
title: "Don't hand me your slop"
date: "Nov 2026"
---

A skill that has become more valuable in the era of Gen-AI is boiling things down to their essence. 

AI agents are currently prone to doing too much. They over-engineer code, and output verbose text. They often do much more than instructed, and this makes reviewing AI generated output cumbersome. Our lazy brains have a tendency to simply accept the extra bits, with the rationale of 'there is no harm having that extra bit here'. Our penchant for loss aversion also plays a role here. Deleting extra bits that could come in handy at some point feels wrong. 

But handing bloated output to another human is inconsiderate. The other person is now forced to weed through the slop and extract the essential bits. Given that their brains are lazy as well, they resort to asking another AI to summarize the output for them. What comes out is the first person's true intent, now mangled by two different LLM passes. 

Working with LLMs needs new etiquette. A great tell for slop is something that takes you less time to generate than it takes for somebody to read it. The principle is then to never subject a human being to this experience. 

But until that etiquette is established, communicating the essence will be a rare and valuable skill.
